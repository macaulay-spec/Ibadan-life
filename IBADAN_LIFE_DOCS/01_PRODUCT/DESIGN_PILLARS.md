# DESIGN PILLARS – IBADAN LIFE

**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Active (Tier 0)  
**Owner:** Design Authority  

These six pillars are non-negotiable. Every feature, system, and design decision must support at least one of them and may never actively undermine any of them.

---

## 1. Physical Presence

The player controls a fully realised 3D character that exists in continuous space.  
Walking, running, driving, entering buildings, and interacting with the environment must feel tangible.  
The city is not a menu or a map of icons — it is a place you move through with your body.

**Test:** Can the player feel the weight and freedom of existing inside the world?

---

## 2. Player-First Population

Real human players are the primary population of the city.  
NPCs exist only to support essential services, ambient life, traffic, police, and specific world functions.  
Social life, economic activity, and meaningful relationships are designed around real players.

**Test:** If all NPCs disappeared, would the city still feel alive with players?

---

## 3. Interconnected Player Economy

Players create value for other players.  
A player can own a restaurant, another can work there, another can buy food, another can deliver, another can supply ingredients, another can advertise.  
The economy is a web of player interdependence, not a series of isolated PvE money faucets.

**Test:** Does this system create meaningful dependency between players?

---

## 4. Authentic Ibadan Texture

The world, culture, humour, architecture, transport, social norms, and daily rhythms must feel specifically like Ibadan (and broader contemporary Nigerian urban life).  
This is not a generic “African city” and not a Lagos clone.  
Cultural authenticity is a design requirement, not decoration.

**Test:** Would someone who knows Ibadan recognise the soul of the place?

---

## 5. Believable Life Progression

Players progress through natural life systems:  
arrival → shelter → income → spending → skills & relationships → better housing/vehicles → business ownership → reputation → deeper civic and economic participation.

Progression should feel earned and continuous, not gated behind arbitrary levels or battle-pass trackers.

**Test:** Does the player’s story feel like a life, or like a checklist?

---

## 6. Mobile-First Reality

Every design decision must respect the constraints and opportunities of mid-range Android devices common in the target market.  
Performance, battery, thermal, network, and touch controls are first-class design concerns from day one.  
We do not design for high-end PC and then “port down.”

**Test:** Does this feature remain enjoyable and stable on a typical mid-range Android phone?

---

## Usage Rule

When evaluating any proposed feature or system, ask:

1. Which pillars does it strengthen?
2. Does it weaken any pillar?
3. If it weakens a pillar, is the trade-off explicitly accepted via ADR?
