> **North Star:** design for the real person holding a mid-range Android phone in a Nigerian city.

---

## Primary Audience

**Nigerian urban youth and young adults (16–35)** who:

- Own **mid-range Android smartphones**
- Know or are curious about life-simulation and open-world games
- Have experienced (or heard of) **Lagos Life** or similar cultural games
- Want a digital space that reflects **their reality, humour, aspirations, and environment**
- Value **social status, hustle, relationships, and visible progress**

---

## Secondary Audiences

- **Nigerian diaspora** who want a living connection to home
- **Other West African** players
- **Global players** who love culturally specific multiplayer experiences

---

## Audience Insights → Design Implications

| Insight | Design implication |
|---------|--------------------|
| **Data costs and device performance matter enormously** | Aggressive optimisation; smart streaming; small, efficient builds |
| **Social visibility and status are powerful motivators** | Reputation, property, vehicles, and businesses must be **visible** |
| **Many play in short sessions** | Meaningful progress must be possible in **20–40 minute** sessions |
| **Trust and safety are critical** in a player-driven social economy | Strong moderation and reporting **from day one** |
| **Cultural humour and recognition create attachment** | Authenticity is a **retention feature**, not a skin |
| **Hustle is a identity, not a mechanic** | The game must respect ambition — earning, owning, building status |

---

## Out of Primary Scope

- Hardcore Western open-world players seeking pure GTA-style chaos
- Players who only want single-player narrative
- High-end PC-only audiences as the design driver

---

## The Player We Design For (Persona)

> **"Ada, 22, Ibadan.** She has a mid-range Tecno. She plays on danfo rides and at night. She wants a game that feels like *her* city — not a Western city with African paint. She wants to hustle, to be recognised, to build something. She has 30 minutes and she wants it to matter."
